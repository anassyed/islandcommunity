The Island: Building Our Community
Description:
Our community sea journey took an unexpected turn. A sudden storm has left us shipwrecked, but by the grace of Allah, we are all safe on an uninhabited island.
We must organize not only to survive, but to build a thriving, faith-driven society. This will take all kinds of people—planners, explorers, artists, healers, builders, and leaders.
The following 12 questions present different tasks and roles that need to be filled. For each question, please choose the **one** you would be **MOST** drawn to do and the **one** you would **LEAST** want to do.

The 12-Question "Island Survival" Test (Final Version)
Question 1
Title: Question 1: The First 24 Hours
Columns:
Finalizing the schedule for who takes night watch and documenting it for everyone to see. 
Leading an exploration team to hike inland and map the island's features, like fresh water sources. 
Planning and leading simple games for the younger survivors to keep them calm and create a sense of normalcy. 
Organizing a daily "fitness" team (coconut soccer, beach running) to keep everyone active and in good shape. 
Question 2
Title: Question 2: First Week Priorities
Columns:
Attending a "how-to" session led by another survivor to learn a new skill (e.g., how to tie survival knots). 
Hosting a low-key "story time" around the first campfire, with no agenda other than for people to share stories and connect. 
Coordinating a 3-hour shift to sit with, comfort, and care for those who are injured or sick. 
Finding a clean, quiet space and organizing the daily communal prayers (Salāh) for the group.
Question 3
Title: Question 3: Establishing the Camp
Columns:
Leading the main "council" meeting to decide on the top 3 survival priorities for the entire group. 
Taking charge of the salvaged tools, repairing broken items, and organizing a "workshop" area. 
Writing a "song of the island" or a poem to perform at the campfire to build a unique community culture. 
Designing a "barter" system, so people can trade tasks (e.g., "I'll watch your kids if you gather firewood"). 
Question 4
Title: Question 4: Building a Foundation
Columns:
Managing the "check-in" desk for all salvaged items, creating a detailed inventory list for the community. 
Inviting a guest speaker to teach the community about their area of expertise (like first-aid or local plants). 
Developing the "rules" for the camp (e.g., how to ration food fairly) and helping everyone agree to them. 
Planning a community picnic day at a local park, including finding the best location and a rain date. 
Question 5
Title: Question 5: Morale & Community
Columns:
Organizing a community-wide potluck dinner where everyone brings a dish to share. 
Figuring out how to build a better shelter, a water filter, or a fishing net from salvaged parts and vines. 
Supervising a "play zone" or craft table during a larger community event. 
Organizing a "community cleanup" day to pick up trash at a local park or neighborhood. 
Question 6
Title: Question 6: Long-Term Faith & Order
Columns:
Organizing a "talent show" or a night of sung poetry (nasheeds) to entertain the community. 
Joining a friendly, low-stakes sports team for a casual weekend game. 
Leading a short, reflective discussion (halaqa) based on the Qur'an or stories of the Prophets to maintain hope. 
Managing the central store of food/water and ensuring the rationing system is fair and being followed. 
Question 7
Title: Question 7: Governance & Society
Columns:
Writing down the "official" camp rules and council decisions to post them for all to see. 
Setting up a casual "board game night" or "trivia night" for small groups to mingle. 
Carving decorations for the communal shelter or using charcoal to create art that tells the story of the island. 
Helping to set up and run a "story time" or a short, family-friendly performance. 
Question 8
Title: Question 8: Skills & Development
Columns:
Taking a "how-to" class with other community members (like a cooking class or a first-aid certification). 
Spending the day carving new tools (like bowls or spoons) or weaving baskets and rope from island fibers. 
Setting up a small, fun "sports day" with simple games (like a frisbee toss or a relay race). 
Deciding the "value" of rare salvaged items (like medicine or batteries) and creating a fair system for their distribution. 
Question 9
Title: Question 9: The Bigger Picture
Columns:
Making the final decision on *where* to build the long-term camp, based on all the exploration reports. 
Running a donation drive (for food, coats, or school supplies) for a local charity. 
Leading a guided "discovery" walk through a nearby nature preserve. 
Taking the time to provide one-on-one spiritual and moral support (nasiha) to those who are feeling hopeless or afraid. 
Question 10
Title: Question 10: A New Normal
Columns:
Organizing the master spreadsheet (on a salvaged laptop) for the camp's inventory and task roster. 
Keeping the "master account" of all food gathered vs. food consumed, ensuring the camp's "budget" is balanced. 
Volunteering to lead a group in a shared moment of Dhikr (remembrance) or reading dua (supplication). 
Watching an informative documentary (on a shared device) and then having a Q&A session with an expert. 
Question 11
Title: Question 11: Community Life
Columns:
Organizing a group outing for a day at the beach or a lake. 
Helping to serve meals at a local soup kitchen for an afternoon. 
Starting the "official" written journal or book that will tell the story of the survivors. 
Leading a morning group (like a running club or a group yoga session) for anyone who wants to join. 
Question 12
Title: Question 12: The Future
Columns:
Organizing a simple, fun activity for children (like a "build a sandcastle" contest) at a community gathering. 
Thinking about the "1-year plan" and what the community will need to survive the next rainy season. 
Designing and leading the construction of a major camp project, like a raised storehouse or a small boat. 
Simply making sure to welcome new faces at an event and introducing them to others.
